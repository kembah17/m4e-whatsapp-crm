## 1. INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Purpose of This Policy

This Privacy Policy ("Policy") describes how personal data is collected, used, stored, shared, and protected in connection with the Marketing4Effect Customer Reactivation Manager platform (the "Platform"), accessible at crm.marketing4effect.com.

This Policy applies to all personal data processed through the Platform, including customer contact information, purchase history, engagement data, and communications data processed on behalf of businesses ("Clients") that use the Platform for customer database reactivation and win-back services.

### 1.2 Scope

This Policy applies to:

- **End Customers** — Individuals whose personal data is uploaded to and processed through the Platform by Clients for the purposes of customer reactivation and engagement.
- **Client Users** — Individuals who access and operate the Platform on behalf of a Client organisation.
- **Website Visitors** — Individuals who visit crm.marketing4effect.com or marketing4effect.com.

### 1.3 Commitment to Data Protection

Marketing4Effect ("M4E") is committed to protecting the privacy and security of all personal data processed through the Platform. M4E processes personal data in compliance with the Nigeria Data Protection Regulation (NDPR) 2019, the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023, and, where applicable, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) of the European Union.

## 5. PURPOSES OF DATA PROCESSING

### 5.1 Primary Purposes

Personal data is processed through the Platform for the following primary purposes:

**(a) Customer Database Reactivation** — Identifying Dormant Customers within the Client's database and executing targeted reactivation campaigns to re-engage them and drive repeat purchases.

**(b) Customer Segmentation** — Classifying End Customers into actionable segments based on purchase recency, customer value, and engagement history to enable personalised communication strategies.

**(c) Multi-Channel Campaign Delivery** — Sending personalised reactivation messages to End Customers via email (Brevo), WhatsApp (Meta Business API), and SMS (Brevo) on behalf of the Client.

**(d) Performance Tracking and Attribution** — Measuring campaign effectiveness by tracking engagement events and attributing reactivated purchases to specific campaigns within the agreed Attribution Window.

**(e) Reporting and Analytics** — Generating performance reports, calculating key metrics (Reactivation Rate, Average Order Value, Customer Lifetime Value, Reactivation Revenue), and providing actionable insights to the Client.

**(f) Deal and Pipeline Management** — Tracking sales opportunities and customer interactions within the CRM pipeline on behalf of the Client.

### 5.2 Secondary Purposes

**(a) Service Improvement** — Using anonymised, aggregated data to improve the Platform's algorithms, segmentation models, and campaign optimisation capabilities.

**(b) Compliance and Legal Obligations** — Processing personal data as necessary to comply with legal obligations, respond to lawful requests from authorities, and establish, exercise, or defend legal claims.

**(c) Security and Fraud Prevention** — Monitoring Platform access and usage to detect and prevent unauthorised access, data breaches, and fraudulent activity.

### 5.3 Prohibited Purposes

M4E shall NOT process End Customer personal data for:

- Selling or renting personal data to third parties
- Advertising or marketing M4E's own services to End Customers
- Profiling End Customers for purposes unrelated to the Client's reactivation campaigns
- Any purpose not authorised by the Client in the Database Reactivation Service Agreement

## 6. LAWFUL BASIS FOR PROCESSING

### 6.1 Lawful Basis Framework

Under the NDPR and NDPA, personal data must be processed on a lawful basis. The applicable lawful basis depends on the category of Data Subject and the nature of the processing:

| Processing Activity | Data Subject Category | Lawful Basis | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reactivation campaigns to existing customers | End Customers (with prior purchase history) | **Legitimate Interest** (NDPR Art. 2.2; NDPA s.25(1)(f)) | The Client has a legitimate interest in re-engaging existing customers who have a prior commercial relationship. The processing is necessary for this purpose and does not override the End Customer's rights, given the pre-existing relationship and the ability to opt out. |
| Marketing communications to new prospects | End Customers (no prior purchase) | **Consent** (NDPR Art. 2.3; NDPA s.25(1)(a)) | Where the End Customer has no prior purchase history, explicit consent is required before sending marketing communications. Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous. |
| Customer segmentation and analytics | End Customers | **Legitimate Interest** (NDPR Art. 2.2; NDPA s.25(1)(f)) | Segmentation of existing customers based on purchase behaviour is a reasonable expectation within the commercial relationship and is necessary for effective service delivery. |
| Transaction data processing for attribution | End Customers | **Performance of Contract** (NDPA s.25(1)(b)) | Processing transaction data is necessary for the performance of the service agreement between M4E and the Client, which includes attribution-based performance fee calculation. |
| Platform access and security monitoring | Client Users | **Legitimate Interest** (NDPA s.25(1)(f)) | Security monitoring is necessary to protect the Platform, personal data, and the interests of all parties. |
| Cookie-based analytics | Website Visitors | **Consent** (where required) | Non-essential cookies require consent. Essential cookies are processed under Legitimate Interest. |

### 6.2 Legitimate Interest Assessment

Where Legitimate Interest is relied upon as the lawful basis, M4E and the Client have conducted a Legitimate Interest Assessment (LIA) considering:

**(a) Purpose Test** — Is there a legitimate interest? Yes — re-engaging existing customers to drive repeat business is a recognised commercial interest.

**(b) Necessity Test** — Is the processing necessary for that purpose? Yes — personalised, data-driven reactivation campaigns require processing of customer contact and transaction data.

**(c) Balancing Test** — Does the legitimate interest override the Data Subject's rights and freedoms? No — the processing involves existing customers with a prior commercial relationship, the data processed is limited to what is necessary, robust security measures are in place, and End Customers can opt out at any time.

### 6.3 Consent Management

Where consent is the applicable lawful basis:

**(a)** The Client is responsible for obtaining valid consent from End Customers before sharing their personal data with M4E for marketing purposes.

**(b)** Consent must meet the requirements of NDPR/NDPA: freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous, demonstrated by a clear affirmative action.

**(c)** End Customers must be able to withdraw consent at any time, with the same ease as it was given. Withdrawal of consent does not affect the lawfulness of processing based on consent before its withdrawal.

**(d)** The Platform provides opt-out mechanisms in all marketing communications, including:
- Unsubscribe links in emails
- "STOP" keyword processing in WhatsApp and SMS messages
- Opt-out request handling through the Client's customer service channels

## 7. DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS

### 7.1 Rights Under NDPR/NDPA

Data Subjects (End Customers, Client Users, and Website Visitors) have the following rights under the NDPR and NDPA:

**(a) Right of Access** — The right to obtain confirmation as to whether personal data concerning them is being processed, and if so, to access that personal data and receive information about the processing (NDPA s.34).

**(b) Right to Rectification** — The right to have inaccurate personal data corrected and incomplete personal data completed (NDPA s.35).

**(c) Right to Erasure (Right to Be Forgotten)** — The right to have personal data erased where: the data is no longer necessary for the purpose for which it was collected; consent is withdrawn; the Data Subject objects to processing; or the data has been unlawfully processed (NDPA s.36).

**(d) Right to Restriction of Processing** — The right to restrict processing where: the accuracy of the data is contested; the processing is unlawful; M4E no longer needs the data but the Data Subject requires it for legal claims; or the Data Subject has objected to processing pending verification (NDPA s.37).

**(e) Right to Data Portability** — The right to receive personal data in a structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format, and to transmit that data to another controller (NDPA s.38).

**(f) Right to Object** — The right to object to processing based on legitimate interest or for direct marketing purposes. Where the Data Subject objects to direct marketing, the personal data shall no longer be processed for that purpose (NDPA s.39).

**(g) Right Not to Be Subject to Automated Decision-Making** — The right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects or similarly significantly affects the Data Subject (NDPA s.40).

### 7.2 Exercising Data Subject Rights

**(a) End Customers** — Because the Client is the Data Controller, End Customer rights requests should be directed to the Client in the first instance. The Client may then instruct M4E to take the necessary technical actions within the Platform (e.g., data export, deletion, or restriction).

**(b) Direct Requests to M4E** — If an End Customer contacts M4E directly with a rights request, M4E shall:
1. Acknowledge receipt within five (5) business days;
2. Notify the relevant Client within two (2) business days;
3. Assist the Client in responding to the request within the statutory timeframe;
4. Not act on the request without the Client's instruction, unless required by law.

**(c) Client Users** — Client Users may exercise their rights by contacting M4E directly at the contact details provided in Section 15.

### 7.3 Response Timeframes

| Right | Response Timeframe | Extension |
|---|---|---|
| Access | 30 days from receipt of valid request | Up to 60 days for complex requests (with notification) |
| Rectification | 30 days from receipt of valid request | Up to 60 days for complex requests |
| Erasure | 30 days from receipt of valid request | Up to 60 days for complex requests |
| Restriction | Without undue delay | — |
| Portability | 30 days from receipt of valid request | Up to 60 days for complex requests |
| Objection | Without undue delay | — |

### 7.4 Verification of Identity

Before processing any Data Subject rights request, M4E or the Client (as applicable) may require verification of the Data Subject's identity to prevent unauthorised access to personal data. Verification may include:
- Confirmation of identity via email address on file
- Provision of government-issued identification (for sensitive requests)
- Confirmation of account details known only to the Data Subject

## 13. DATA BREACH NOTIFICATION

### 13.1 Definition of Data Breach

A "personal data breach" means a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed through the Platform.

### 13.2 Notification to the Client (Data Controller)

In the event of a personal data breach affecting End Customer data:

**(a)** M4E shall notify the Client without undue delay and in any event within **seventy-two (72) hours** of becoming aware of the breach, in accordance with NDPR requirements.

**(b)** The notification shall include:
- A description of the nature of the breach, including the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects and personal data records affected;
- The name and contact details of M4E's data protection contact;
- A description of the likely consequences of the breach;
- A description of the measures taken or proposed to address the breach, including measures to mitigate its possible adverse effects.

**(c)** Where it is not possible to provide all information simultaneously, information may be provided in phases without undue further delay.

### 13.3 Notification to the NDPC

The Client, as Data Controller, is responsible for notifying the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) of a personal data breach within seventy-two (72) hours where the breach is likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of Data Subjects. M4E shall cooperate with and assist the Client in making such notification.

### 13.4 Notification to Data Subjects

Where a personal data breach is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of Data Subjects, the Client shall communicate the breach to the affected Data Subjects without undue delay. M4E shall assist the Client in identifying affected Data Subjects and preparing appropriate communications.

### 13.5 Breach Response Procedures

M4E maintains documented data breach response procedures, including:

1. **Detection and Containment** — Immediate steps to identify the scope of the breach and contain further data exposure.
2. **Assessment** — Evaluation of the nature, scope, and severity of the breach, including the categories of data and Data Subjects affected.
3. **Notification** — Timely notification to the Client, NDPC, and Data Subjects as required.
4. **Remediation** — Implementation of measures to address the breach and prevent recurrence.
5. **Documentation** — Recording of all breaches, including facts, effects, and remedial actions taken, regardless of whether notification was required.
